Oakdale's planning commission meets Thursday night on 55 rental townhomes at a former Par 3 the city's plan still guides as open space.

Mulcahy Holdings LLC wants the units at 5585 Golfview Avenue North, the old Oakdale Par 3, parcel 0602921230013. The report says the site was also a firearms range. The ask is a comprehensive plan amendment, a planned unit development amendment, a preliminary plat, and a site plan. Staff recommend approval of each. The commission holds the hearing and recommends to the city council. Council action is expected Oct. 27. A later public hearing is listed for the planned unit development amendment.

The comprehensive plan guides the property as Open Space. Allowed uses in that category are undeveloped publicly owned land and wetlands. The request would re-guide it to Medium Density Residential, 4 to 30 units an acre, which allows townhomes. Proposed density is about 10 units an acre, with protected wetland left out of the count. Zoning is The Greens of Silver Lake planned unit development, which designates the site for open space and recreational use. The amendment would apply R-3 townhome standards.

The parcel is about 44.6 acres. Buildable area is about 5.1 acres. The remainder is about 39.5 acres. A structure and a remnant parking lot are still there. The rest is mostly vacant land, wetland, and trees. Most upland outside the proposed footprint is described as wetland fill authorized by a Department of Natural Resources permit in 1986. Access is from 56th Street North. The townhomes would be tied to Cedric's Landing apartments and share some amenities. The units are proposed as market rate, not income restricted. Engineering confirmed sewer and water capacity for the units.

North of the site is open space and wetland. South and west are low-density housing. East is a manufactured home park.

The plat shows one buildable lot and three outlots, for stormwater, a pedestrian link to Cedric's Landing, and wetland. The developer must drop the outlots and combine them into the one lot.

Private streets would dead-end about 800 feet from 56th Street North and Golfview Avenue North. The code maximum for a dead-end street is 300 feet. Proposed width is 24 feet from back of curb to back of curb. The city standard is 24 feet of pavement, usually measured face of curb to face of curb, so the section is two feet narrower. The developer is asking the council to allow the longer dead end and the narrower street. No other vehicle access is proposed.

Projected traffic is 450 to 550 trips a day, 50 to 60 in the peak hours. The city engineer determined 56th Street North can take that traffic. A traffic study is still required at 56th and Highway 120, also called Century Avenue. That intersection has a northbound-to-eastbound right-turn lane. It has no left-turn lane for southbound-to-eastbound traffic, and no dedicated right-turn lane from 56th onto the highway.

Western townhomes are shown as one story. Eastern townhomes are three. The height limit is 40 feet. R-3 setbacks are 30 feet in front, 5 feet on an interior side, and 30 feet in the rear. The plat shows those met, with townhomes more than 30 feet from the western lines. Impervious cover cannot exceed 75 percent. Less than five acres of the 44.6-acre site are proposed as impervious. The parking rule is 2 spaces a unit plus half a space a unit for visitors. Plans show 24 extra guest spaces. The landscaping plan shows 184 trees, matching the green-space count. Up to 25 percent of significant trees can come out without mandatory replacement.

A September environmental assessment found possible contamination from herbicides, fungicides, pesticides, and fertilizers on former greens and tees. Mercury was detected at three former greens. Lead was detected at the maintenance building. The assessment did not treat the possible former gun range as a recognized environmental condition. Conditions include cleanup to residential standards of the Minnesota Pollution Control Agency and the U.S. Department of Agriculture, and a Limited Phase II assessment.

The application was complete June 22. Review was extended to Dec. 19. A 125-unit apartment concept went to a council workshop on May 10, 2022, and was not pursued. A townhome concept went to the council on March 10.

Mulcahy Holdings has not requested city financial assistance. The developer is responsible for the infrastructure and for cleanup of the contamination the assessment identified. A park dedication fee is due under the city's fee schedule. The agenda does not print the amount. No public park is planned on the property.

The hearing is at 7 at the Oakdale Discovery Center. The next regular meeting is Nov. 5, also at 7, at the same place. Valley Branch Watershed District and Department of Natural Resources comments are pending. Metropolitan Council review follows council approval.
