Oakdale's council meets Tuesday night on a $975,871 street levy that would leave the 2026 budget and come back in 2027.

That figure is debt-issuance levy money in the Streets Capital Improvement Fund for street projects R2026-01 and R2026-02. The amendment would cut it from 2026 revenue and put the same $975,871 into 2027, as reimbursement for those projects.

A September 22 parameters vote allowed a bond sale of up to $20,000,000, at a true interest cost of no more than 5.5 percent, due February 1, 2053. The restated sale drops the improvement bonds. The new ceiling is $18,600,000: $11,900,000 in sales-tax revenue bonds and $6,700,000 in capital-improvement bonds. The interest cap rises to 6 percent. The maturity date stays February 1, 2053. Bids have to be for at least $17,449,275 plus accrued interest. The proposed sale date is October 22. The city administrator and the finance director would sign the purchase agreement if the best bid stays inside those limits.

Police department expansion and the city hall remodel together are estimated at about $35 million. A 2024-2028 capital plan put the city levy share of remaining police expansion costs at $2 million, and the city hall remodel at $8-10 million in two phases. A hearing on the capital-improvement portion was held July 23, 2024.

Les Schwab Tire Center wants a final plat on a vacant outlot north of 33rd Street North, parcel 1702921410011, with consent of the owner, Oakdale Marketplace Ova LLC. The site is about 7.5 acres, split by 33rd Street North. The buildable piece, about 2.2 acres, would hold an 8,200 square foot building for tire sales and automotive service. The land is in the Oakdale Marketplace planned-unit district, guided mixed use. No variance and no city incentive are requested. The council approved the preliminary plat and site plan on August 25. The development agreement does not state a dollar amount.

Knights of Columbus Council #4374 wants a bingo fundraiser on November 14 at Transfiguration Catholic Church, 6135 15th Street North, with cash and food prizes. City code allows lawful gambling when the council approves it.

Pavilion painting is listed at $37,705, from Top-Tier Professional Painting. A second quote is $46,045. A public-safety traffic grant is listed at $19,750. An amendment to the utility rate study is $9,900, bringing that agreement to $109,400. A T-Mobile lease on Water Tower Three at 2347 Hallmark Avenue North would run to January 31, 2053. The current lease ends January 30, 2028. A payment register in the packet totals $2,315,795.55. The agenda does not say what period that register covers.

The meeting is at 7 p.m. at the Discovery Center, 4444 Hadley Avenue North. Speakers at the open forum get three minutes.
