Stillwater's city council meets Tuesday night on a Myrtle Street reconstruction estimated at $4,570,142 and a $500 service charge on downtown commercial buildings.

The Myrtle Street Improvement Project runs from Owens Street North to Main Street North, 0.72 miles. The feasibility report puts the total at $4,570,142, including design, surveying, inspection, administration, and contingencies. Assessments on benefiting properties total $670,142, or 35 percent under the assessment policy, with the city funding the other 65 percent. A state LRIP grant covers $1,400,000 and state aid funds cover $2,500,000. Residential owners would pay $7,804 per unit. Corner lots count as half a unit per frontage. Duplexes and condominiums count as 0.8 units. Commercial, city, and church-owned properties would pay $185 per adjusted linear foot. An appraisal estimated a $12,000 benefit per residential unit. The council will hold a public hearing and consider a resolution to order the improvements.

Downtown, the Special Service District would collect $50,500 for services, or $500 per commercial building inside the district. One property is listed at $1,000. The charges are payable with 2026 taxes in one installment at zero interest. The payment deadline is November 16, 2026; any remaining balance goes to the county as a special assessment. Only commercial property in the district is subject to the charges. Staff recommends holding a public hearing and adopting the assessments.

Tax Increment Financing District #8, decertified as of December 31, 2022, has $291,897.91 in excess increment. Staff recommends remitting it to Washington County for redistribution to local taxing entities.

A zoning text amendment for accessory structures in the Traditional Residential district is on for first reading. The planning commission unanimously recommended it on October 28. Staff says it would let larger lots put up bigger structures, up to 1,000 square feet, while limiting the effect on average TR properties. One example is a parcel at 12210 McKusick Road.

The Greater Stillwater Chamber of Commerce seeks an agreement for the 2027 World Snow Celebration at North Lowell Park, January 11 to 17. Estimated fees total $39,150, weather dependent, covering application, setup, base fees, several parking lots, water for snowmaking, alcohol permits, food vendors, liquor licenses, and a security deposit already on file. The chamber asks to waive all of them. The council would also consider a noise variance and a temporary liquor license.

An agreement for a 2027 ice rink in Parking Lot 4, from December 21, 2026, to March 22, 2027, includes a request to waive a $100 special event fee and $18,400 in lot rental for 92 days, or $18,500 total. A $1,000 security deposit is listed.

The Ramsey/Washington Recycling & Energy Center tipping fee for acceptable waste would be $159.00 per ton in 2027, a 12 percent increase from 2026. An official notice is on the agenda under council request items.

The meeting is scheduled for 7:00 p.m. on November 4.
