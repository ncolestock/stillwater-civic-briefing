## What the record says

Oakdale Planning Commission is scheduled Thursday, Oct. 1, 2026, at 7:00 p.m. at the Oakdale Discovery Center. The next regular meeting is listed for Nov. 5, 2026, at 7 p.m. at the same place.

New business is a public hearing on Cedric's Crossing, 5585 Golfview Avenue North and parcel 0602921230013: a comprehensive plan amendment, a planned unit development amendment, a preliminary plat, and a site plan. The agenda recommends approval of each. The planning commission report says the role of the commission is to hold the hearing and recommend to the city council. Expected city council action is Oct. 27, 2026. A later public hearing is listed for the PUD amendment.

Mulcahy Holdings LLC, the property owner, proposes a 55-unit rental townhome development at the former Oakdale Par 3 golf course. The report says the site was also previously a firearms range. The townhomes are proposed to be associated with Cedric's Landing apartments and will share some amenities. The units are proposed to be market rate, not income restricted.

The site is parcel 0602921230013 and 5585 Golfview Avenue North, about 44.6 acres. Buildable area for the townhomes is about 5.1 acres; the remainder is about 39.5 acres. The site contains a structure and remnant parking lot of the Par 3 course, and is mostly vacant land with delineated wetland and trees. Most upland areas outside the proposed footprint are described as wetland fill authorized by a Department of Natural Resources permit in 1986. The development checklist lists existing site conditions as vacant golf course; wetland. Access is listed as 56th Street N.

The comprehensive plan guides the property as Open Space. Allowed uses in that category are printed as undeveloped publicly owned land and wetlands. The request is to re-guide it to Medium Density Residential. That category is printed as a density range of 4 to 30 units per acre and allows townhomes. Proposed density is approximately 10 units per acre, with protected wetland excluded from the calculation. Engineering confirmed sufficient sewer and water capacity for the proposed units.

Zoning is The Greens of Silver Lake Planned Unit Development, which designates the site for open space and recreational use. The PUD amendment would apply R-3 townhome dimensional standards. Surrounding designations in the report table: north, Open Space / PUD / recreation, wetland; south, Low Density Residential / PUD / residential; east, Mixed Use / R-5 Manufactured Home Park / residential; west, Low Density Residential / PUD / residential.

The preliminary plat shows one buildable lot and three outlots (Outlot A stormwater, Outlot B pedestrian connection to Cedric's Landing, Outlot C wetland). Staff say the developer must update the plat to eliminate the three outlots and combine them into Lot 1. Access would be via 56th Street North, private streets, no additional vehicular access. The private street network dead ends and is approximately 800 feet from the intersection of 56th Street North and Golfview Avenue North. Proposed street width is 24 feet from back-of-curb to back-of-curb. The city standard for private roads is 24 feet of pavement, usually a face-of-curb measurement, so the report says the proposed section is two feet narrower than the standard. The developer is seeking council approval to exceed the 300-foot maximum length for dead-end streets (City of Oakdale, Code of Ordinances, Chapter 21, Division 21-04-300(k)) and to build streets two feet narrower than standard.

Projected trips: 450-550 vehicle trips per day, 50-60 in peak hours. The city engineer determined 56th Street North can accommodate the added traffic. Additional analysis is required at 56th and Highway 120/Century Avenue. The report says there is a northbound-to-eastbound right turn lane on Highway 120/Century Avenue, no left turn lane for southbound-to-eastbound traffic, and no dedicated right turn lane from 56th Street North to Highway 120/Century Avenue North. The developer must commission a traffic study of that intersection.

R-3 setbacks printed for townhomes: 30 feet from the front lot line, 5 feet from the interior side lot lines, 30 feet from the rear lot line. The plat shows setbacks met, townhomes over 30 feet from the western property lines. Building height limit is 40 feet. Western townhomes are shown as one story; eastern townhomes as three stories. Impervious coverage cannot exceed 75 percent. The report says the requirement is met because the site is about 44.6 acres and less than five acres are proposed as impervious. Parking rule printed as 2 spaces per unit plus 1/2 space per unit for visitor parking. Plans show 24 additional guest spaces. Tree rule: 25 percent of significant trees can be removed without mandatory replacement; one tree per 1,000 square feet of green space, 184 trees required, and the landscaping plan shows 184 trees.

A Phase I environmental site assessment completed in September 2026 identified potential contamination from herbicides, fungicides, pesticides, and fertilizers on former green and tee areas. Mercury was detected at three former golf greens. Lead was detected at the maintenance building. The assessment concluded that the possible former gun range is not considered a recognized environmental condition. Staff conditions include cleanup to residential standards of the Minnesota Pollution Control Agency and the United States Department of Agriculture, and a Limited Phase II ESA.

Application deemed complete June 22, 2026. 60-day review deadline August 21, 2026. Extension granted to December 19, 2026. A concept plan for a 125-unit apartment building was presented at a council workshop May 10, 2022, and was not pursued. A townhome concept was presented to the council March 10, 2026.

The packet also includes minutes of the Sept. 3, 2026, planning commission meeting. Those minutes cover a different case (Hadley Storage, 6815 55th Street North) and are not this hearing.

## Dollars

Amount not in the document for a park dedication fee. The report says the developer must provide a park dedication fee in accordance with the city's adopted fee schedule, and that no public park is planned on the property.

"The applicant has not requested financial assistance from the City for this development." The checklist answers "No" to financial incentives and to 4d status.

No sale price, contract amount, or city expenditure is printed. The developer is responsible for infrastructure and for remediation of identified contamination.

## Votes

Not yet voted. Staff recommend approval of the comprehensive plan amendment, PUD amendment, preliminary plat, and site plan with the conditions in the draft resolutions and ordinance. Draft motions are to recommend approval of each.
