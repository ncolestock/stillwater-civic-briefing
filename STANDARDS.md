# Writing standard

Draft for Nathan to mark up. The automations follow this file.

## What a story is

A story tells a neighbor what a public record says happened, or what a meeting is being asked to do. The record does the talking. The writing makes that record readable.

The first two sentences carry the body, the place, and the fact that matters. A dollar, a charge, a vote, a street, a time. Then stop.

## Sound

- Short sentences. One fact in each.
- Name the city and the body.
- Use the document's number. Do not round it into a slogan.
- A meeting that has not happened is on the agenda. Say that once.
- If the packet does not contain a number, say it does not. Do not fill the hole with a mood.
- Quotes are rare, short, and exact. If the words are not in the document, there is no quote.
- The same sentence shape does not open every story. "The board has not voted" is not a refrain.
- No "historic," "controversial," "stunning," "embattled," "raises questions," "comes as," "underscores," "community members," or "residents deserve."
- No first person. No host. The paper is not a person.
- The disclaimer sits once, at the bottom. It is not a sentence in the story.

## Length

A booking is a short page. A meeting story is as long as the packet supports, usually under 450 words. A thin record stays thin.

## Blotter

One adult, one page. The blotter list is the archive and it keeps every page.

Say who was booked, on what date, by which agency, and the charge as the book-in report writes it. Put that charge in a sentence first, then the statute title.

Do not add an address, age, date of birth, officer, arrest location, mugshot, or the word guilty. Leave out juveniles and sex-crime charges.

The sheriff's dispatch calls and incident summaries do not name people. Do not pin one of those rows on a booking. A court case gets a link only after that case has been opened and the name and charge match. Minnesota Court Records Online is https://publicaccess.courts.state.mn.us/.

## Before it is published

Research writes the notes. The writer writes from the notes. Fact-check opens the source again. A claim that is not in the fetched document does not run.
